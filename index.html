Latest · BDI Hackathon 2026

Floodtir — measuring floods from a single photo

A crowdsourcing flood platform for Bangkok, built for the Big Data Institute (BDI) Hackathon 2026. Our team reached the Finalist round — Top 21 teams. I designed the AI model at its core.

Why Bangkok still floods blind

The Drainage and Sewerage Department runs 500+ water-level gauges (248 road + 314 canal), mostly on main roads. But 45% of Bangkok's roads are dead-end sois, and in a basin-shaped city a few tens of centimetres of elevation change the water level a lot. The side streets where people are actually at risk have no data. That gap is what Floodtir closes.

Target user Bangkok Governor — the office overseeing the city's flood management and the Drainage and Sewerage Department

What I did

  • Designed FAHRPF, which measures flood height from one street photo, removing the need for installed water-level sensors.
  • Architected a fully zero-shot pipeline of three foundation models (SAM 3, YOLO26 + SAM 3, Depth Anything V2) with no labelled training data.
  • Formulated the measurement method: everyday objects act as rulers, and a depth- and confidence-weighted average keeps it robust to occlusion and perspective.
  • Benchmarked against a published VLM approach (Gemma 4-E4B, 4B params): 16.01 vs 15.12 cm MAE with 77.8% fewer parameters.
  • Built the evaluation dataset from scratch by sourcing and annotating real Thai flood imagery from news reports.

AI Builders · 2022–23 Showcase Series #67

Will it rain tomorrow? Where my AI journey started

A classifier that predicts tomorrow's rain from 3,249 days × 20 variables of TMD weather-station data (Bang Na, 2014–2022). I revisited it after Super AI Engineer and lifted accuracy from 67% to 82%.

AI Builders · 202367%
Revisited · post Super AI82%
  • Caught a silent data leak: missing days made the model predict 2 days ahead instead of tomorrow. Rebuilt a complete 2014–2022 calendar, left-joined with a 1-day lag, and dropped unpredictable days.
  • Feature engineering: 1- and 2-day lags of total rainfall, minimum pressure and minimum temperature, chosen from DecisionTree feature importance.
  • Physically grounded: rain tends to persist day to day, while temperature and pressure drive the condensation that makes rain.
  • Logistic regression, evaluated on both random and time-ordered train/test splits.
